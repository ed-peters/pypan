# pypan-75: Macro-scale play (epic)

## The pitch

> **Rivals:** 2–3 named AI trading houses competing for the same routes. They respond
> to your moves. Cornering the silk market angers the Jardine & Matheson analog.
>
> **Historical events as pressure:** Opium Wars, typhoons, treaty port openings —
> scripted events that permanently reshape the map.
>
> **Legacy goals:** beyond money, pursue political influence, a merchant fleet
> legacy, or control of a specific commodity.

| Bead | Feature | Related |
|---|---|---|
| pypan-76 | Named rival trading houses | foundation for pypan-61, pypan-62, pypan-54, pypan-71 |
| pypan-77 | Historical events | pypan-57 (new ports), pypan-33 (jade event) |
| pypan-78 | Legacy goals | the score, pypan-59 |

## How the pitch fits this game

- **Rivals keep coming up.** They're the missing piece in several existing beads:
  - pypan-61: rivals strike back at your holdings
  - pypan-62: the squeeze makes enemies
  - pypan-54: the traders who borrow from you
  - pypan-71: exclusive relationships

  **pypan-76 is the shared foundation.** Building it once avoids four separate
  half-models of "other merchants".
- **The game already has a calendar:** it starts in 1860, and voyages advance it by a
  month. That makes **scripted events by year** easy to place. But the game runs
  without limit, so a script would eventually run out.
- **The only goal is the score:** cash + bank − debt, per month played. Retiring
  (allowed only when out of debt) ends the game with a rank. Legacy goals are a
  second measure of success.

## Recommendation

1. **pypan-76 first:** it unblocks or improves four other beads. Start with an
   **abstract** model: each house has wealth, a favorite good and port, and an
   attitude toward you. Real AI ships sailing around are unnecessary.
2. **pypan-77 next:** a small event framework (date, conditions, effects), seeded with
   a handful of events. Treaty ports (pypan-57) and the jade mine (pypan-33) can be
   events in it.
3. **pypan-78 last:** goals are only interesting once there's influence, holdings and
   rivals to have goals about.

## Questions that cut across the epic

1. **How much of this is "Taipan" any more?** Together with pypan-59, this turns a
   simple trading game into a strategy game. Is that the direction, or should these
   stay optional (an "Empire mode" start choice)?
2. **Historical accuracy:** real names and events, or close fictional analogs? The
   pitch says "the Jardine & Matheson analog", which suggests analogs.
3. **Is there an end?** If history is scripted, does the game end at a date (for
   example 1900), or keep going after the script runs out?
